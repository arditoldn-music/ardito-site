The Artist

Omjine returned for her second release under Ardito's management, this time with a working relationship already in place. Precious One had given both sides a sense of what a campaign actually involves, what she's comfortable with content-wise, and where the gaps in her own setup were.

KOMCA registration was already sorted from the first campaign. This time the groundwork was pitching infrastructure and lead time, not artist admin.

The Brief

Sweethearting is about being told you're too much by someone who keeps charming you anyway. The refrain, "how dramatic, sweethearting me," carries the whole idea, someone playing along with their own criticism because the attention is still worth it. Sonically it moves toward Yeah Yeah Yeahs and The xx, a shift from Precious One's more sparse, Cat Power-leaning sound.

Where Precious One was a two week stress test, this campaign had roughly five weeks of lead time. That gap is the throughline of everything that follows.

What Ardito Did

Full pitches went out to UK radio and blogs, followed by individual Tuesday morning follow-ups rather than a single blast and silence. A separate Instagram DM push ran in parallel, direct outreach to radio stations and playlist curators who don't take email pitches. Around 40 posts went into UK-based Facebook music promotion groups, each one worded differently rather than copy-pasted.

Beyond the standard press list, outreach extended into Omjine's own academic network, old BIMM contacts, a former BIMM lecturer, and BIMM's alumni mailing list. Two Mailchimp sends went to her existing list, one pre-release, one on release day.

Results

Sweethearting has 232 streams from 68 listeners since release on 10 July. That is behind Precious One on raw streams (397 from 58 listeners), but the people who found it kept it: 32 saves and 31 playlist adds, against 26 and 19. Playlist adds count listeners adding the song to their own playlists as well as curator lists, so they are not placements, but 31 adds from 68 listeners is a strong ratio for a release this size. Streams sit well below Spotify's 1,000-in-28-days threshold for display ads, which is its own result and is covered below. No ad budget was spent, so everything here came from outreach.

Unlike Precious One, this campaign landed real coverage. Amazing Radio gave it UK and USA airplay, a First Spin, and a top 3 place on the Audition Poll. Amazing Indie streamed it. BBC Introducing's upload was listened to by programming. Radio Wigwam, FBi Radio in Australia, and Gecko Radio all played it. A Korean Indie outlet ran a release-day feature, and ByteFM replied positively to a pitch written in German.

Free playlist submissions through DailyPlaylists also paid off after release. Two curators approved the track and a third offered to add it on request. It was a small result, but it cost nothing and is now a repeatable step for future releases.

Listener geography also told its own story. London led the way, but Seoul, Kuala Lumpur, and Singapore all showed up in the top cities, a direct reflection of Omjine's own background rather than a generic UK indie audience.

What Ardito Learned

Lead time changes what's possible.

Precious One's 15 pitches got zero replies. This campaign, run with roughly five weeks instead of two, landed seven confirmed wins across radio and press. Same artist, same pitching quality, different runway. The extra coverage did not turn into more streams though. Precious One still leads on plays, so press and radio wins build a profile and a contact list but do not guarantee listeners.

Press photo is still the bottleneck, just a smaller one.

Only Instagram photos were available this time round rather than a proper shoot. Workable, but not ideal, and confirmed early rather than discovered mid-campaign like last time.

Follower count gates more than people expect.

Spotify's display ad tools need 1,000 streams in 28 days and Marquee needs 5,000 monthly listeners. An artist at this stage simply can't access either yet, regardless of campaign quality. Meta ads were the only paid channel actually available, and in the end none was spent.

Radio wins can happen with zero paid spend.

Every confirmed placement on this campaign came from pitching, not advertising. No ad budget was spent at all.

Facebook music groups barely moved anything.

Around 40 posts, each worded differently, mostly got a single like or none. Those groups are full of artists posting their own tracks, not listeners. Direct, individual outreach did far more, so this channel has been dropped from future plans.

What Comes Next

Soundplate, BIRP, and Indiemono submissions are still pending, queued for once momentum from this write-up settles. Meta ads did not run on this release, so paid promotion is still untested for Omjine.
