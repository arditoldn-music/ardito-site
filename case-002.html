ARD 002 · Case study

Omjine · sweethearting

The second campaign, with the five weeks of lead time. That's the most valuable asset which the first single release didn't have.

Listen on Spotify ↗See Omjine's post ↗

The brief

The song is about being told you're too much by someone who keeps charming you anyway. It moves towards Yeah Yeah Yeahs and The xx, away from the sparse sound of Precious One. This time there was time to run the campaign properly.

What I did

I sent out full pitches to UK radio and blogs, then individual follow-ups instead of one blast and silence. I also tracked what times would be best in terms of getting responses. Followed this by a separate Instagram DM push to stations and curators who don't take email. Outreach into Omjine's own network at BIMM, and two Mailchimp sends to her list.

Pitched to Amazing Radio, BBC Introducing, Soho Radio, Radio Wigwam, Fresh on the Net, DIY, Stereogum, Crack, Gigslutz, Happy Mag, ByteFM, FBi Radio and Gecko Radio.

What happened

Amazing Radio gave it UK and US airplay, a First Spin and a top 3 place in the Audition Poll. BBC Introducing's programming team listened to it. Radio Wigwam, FBi Radio in Australia and Gecko Radio played it. A Korean indie outlet ran a release-day feature, and ByteFM replied to a pitch I wrote in German.

Free playlist submissions through DailyPlaylists also paid off after release. Two curators approved the track and a third offered to add it on request. It was a small result, but it cost nothing and is now a repeatable step for future releases.

sweethearting has 232 streams from 68 listeners since release on 10 July. That is behind Precious One on raw streams (397 from 58 listeners), but the people who found it kept it: 32 saves and 31 playlist adds, against 26 and 19. Playlist adds count listeners adding the song to their own playlists as well as curator lists, so they are not all placements, but 31 adds from 68 listeners is a strong ratio for a release this size. Streams are still well below Spotify's threshold for display ads, and no ad budget was spent, so everything here came from outreach and organically.

While the numbers aren't massive, there is a clear shift compared to the Precious One campaign. The interactions this song received went much deeper: people added it to playlists because they connected with it, and the Korean Indie coverage helped establish Omjine as an artist.

What I learned

Lead time changes everything. Zero replies with two weeks vs. seven wins when I had five.

Radio doesn't mean streams. Coverage builds a profile. It doesn't guarantee listeners.

Facebook music groups do NOT work. 40 posts, almost no response apart from some likes on the Facebook post. Dropped.

Every win came from pitching. No money was spent on ads.
